Board P (PD front end) and the LED/control board are separate PCBs per the epic's board-split decision. This page evaluates connector families for that link — power (15V, contract cap 3A) plus whatever status/signal lines the architecture pass ends up needing (e.g. an ATT/PDOK line per the Board-P audit). All parts lookup-verified against the local JLCPCB DB (fetched 2026-07-25; per-part lookup dates noted).

## Candidate 1 — XFCN direct 2.54 mm male/female pair (selected)

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Part | PZ254V-11-06P male (C492405) + PM254V-11-06-H85 female (C2832269) |
| Pitch | 2.54mm |
| Rating | 250V / **3A per contact** on both halves |
| Pins | 6 |
| Stock (tier) | 98,021 male / 42,315 female on 2026-08-20; [male](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/XFCN-PZ254V_1106P/C492405), [female](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/XFCN-PM254V_11_06H85/C2832269) |

The manufacturer drawings explicitly specify the reciprocal PZ254/PM254 single-row series as mates. Power contacts remain doubled (2 of the 6 pins ganged per rail for both the 15V feed and return), leaving 2 pins for signal/status.

**Pros:** cheap, compact, amply stocked, exact manufacturer-matched pair, and no cable housing/contact/wire/crimp BOM.
**Cons:** unkeyed and not latching; PCB alignment, pin-1 polarity, standoff, and mechanical retention become part of the board/enclosure design. A single contact has no headroom over the 3A contract cap, so doubling remains mandatory for that envelope.

## Candidate 2 — JST VH, 2-pin (headroom alternative)

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Part | B2P-VH(LF)(SN) (C160315) |
| Pitch | 3.96mm |
| Rating | 250V / **10A per contact** |
| Pins | 2 |
| Stock (tier) | 207,876, [partdetail](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/C160315) |

VH is the "step up" connector family precisely for cases like this: a single contact carries **3.3× the 3A contract cap** with margin, so no doubling is needed — one pin per rail is enough. A 3-pin (VH-3A / B3P-VH, C160316) or 4-pin (VH-4A / B4P-VH, C160317) variant in the same family would add signal pins without touching the power-margin story.

**Pros:** real single-contact margin over the 3A cap, same connector family scales to more pins if signal lines are added, robust for hand assembly/rework.
**Cons:** larger 3.96mm pitch and taller housing than XH — bigger board-edge footprint; splitting power (VH) and signal (a separate small connector) into two connectors is more parts/more assembly steps than XH's single 6-pin combo, unless a wider VH variant is used for everything.

## Candidate 3 (for comparison) — Molex-style KK 2.54mm, 4-pin

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Part | 2510-4A (C2847671) |
| Pitch | 2.54mm |
| Rating | 250V / **3A** |
| Pins | 4 |
| Stock (tier) | 104,421, [partdetail](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/C2847671) |

Included to show that XH isn't an outlier — most connectors in this 2–2.5mm pitch class top out at 3A per contact, i.e. exactly at this project's cap with zero margin. This family would need the same doubled-contact treatment as XH if selected; it isn't a genuine alternative to the margin problem, just a different footprint with the identical constraint.

## Questions the architecture pass must answer

- Is the selected pair's zero-contract-margin doubling scheme acceptable here, or does the LED board's actual 410 mA load and 205 mA/contact split make VH's extra headroom unnecessary?
- How many signal/status lines actually need to cross this connector (ATT/PDOK status, anything else)? That fixes whether XH's remaining 2 pins are enough, or whether VH needs a companion small-signal connector.
- Board-edge space, direct-board alignment, standoff, polarity, and retention must be fixed in the PCB/enclosure layout before the unkeyed pair is energized.
