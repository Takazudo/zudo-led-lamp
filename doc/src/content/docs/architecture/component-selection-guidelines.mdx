## Draft recommendations: cost and availability first

Start with circuit requirements, then compare a small shortlist of inexpensive,
widely stocked JLCPCB orderables before committing the design to a costly or obscure
part. For ordinary interchangeable parts, match value, package, tolerance, working
voltage, power and dielectric requirements. Prefer Basic when its actual assembly
cost makes the total lower; a tier label alone does not decide suitability or cost.
Do not spend an exhaustive brand comparison on a low-stress ordinary resistor or
hold its source selection for unrelated physical measurements.

Scale the review to function. Precision feedback/sensing, timing, audio-critical
paths, storage/low-leakage nodes and high-stress or ambiguous applications need the
specific relevant limits: for example TCR, voltage coefficient, leakage, noise,
ESR/stability, effective capacitance, pulse energy or thermal limits. Engineering
must derive these from the circuit and exact sources; do not ask the user to invent
limits or silently relax a justified requirement to select a cheaper part.

Before selecting, record exact manufacturer, complete orderable MPN/suffix,
package and pin map, supplier code, quantity basis, source URL and observation date.
Separate catalog listing, current stock, assembly eligibility, lead time and
user-owned inventory. Compare total delivered/assembled cost including minimum
quantity, unavoidable surplus, setup/placement charges, shipping and tax. Preserve
unknown charges explicitly. Recheck dated evidence when a real purchase is prepared;
this draft contains no live stock check or current quote.

## Draft recommendations: compare socket routes explicitly

Compare these routes early when an IC dominates cost. A socket does not itself
reduce the chip price, and a DIP version or pin-compatible adapter is not assumed.

| Route | Identify and count | Actual work and material effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct SMT PCBA | Exact IC, board footprint, fitted quantity and assembler charges | Factory placement/soldering; package heat path and assembly constraints |
| Separately sourced genuine DIP plus socket | Exact genuine DIP orderable, socket pitch/width/pin count, IC/socket quantities and supplier terms | Socket must be soldered by the permitted assembler, then IC inserted; check height/clearance, retention and relevant contact/current or leakage limits |
| Identified SMD adapter plus socket | Exact SMD IC, adapter circuit/pin mapping, adapter PCB/assembly and compatible socket | State who solders IC to adapter and socket to main PCB, and who inserts it; include extra area/height, heat path, relevant parasitics/leakage and contacts |

For a two-finished-unit comparison, start with twice the actual placements of each
IC/socket/adapter, subtract only established reusable inventory, then apply each
supplier's actual MOQ and excess requirements. Keep installed demand and purchase
quantity separate. If the project's final quantity differs, recompute rather than
carry over this comparison scenario.

The user considers insertion effort minor. Do not dismiss substantial verified
savings with a generic labor penalty or hypothetical reliability warning. List the
actual soldering/insertion steps and any circuit-specific effect that changes the
choice; do not assign an hourly cost without evidence or user direction. A
factory-only soldering requirement, where applicable, remains separate from manual
socket insertion: insertion permission does not authorize hand-soldering a socket
or adapter. Check the project's assembly contract before proposing that route.

## Unresolved choices and evidence boundary

No replacement part, socket, DIP variant, adapter, assembler or supplier is selected
by this guidance. Additional parent review may follow; do not invent
prior approvals or describe every costly historical part as a proven mistake.
Keep confirmed user decisions separate from recommendations and inherited engineering
assumptions. A candidate BOM identity does not authorize canonical adoption,
manufacturing or product acceptance. Existing electrical and physical qualification
gates remain attached to the relevant design, not to every unrelated ordinary part.
